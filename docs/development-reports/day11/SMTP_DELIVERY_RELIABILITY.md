# SMTP delivery reliability

A PostgreSQL outbox job has an exclusive expiring lease and a bounded retry count. The notification and job reach their final state in one transaction. The controlled SMTP adapter derives a stable Message-ID from the job ID. Stable IDs correlate attempts and support relay deduplication if the relay provides it. SMTP relay acceptance and the database update are separate commits. A restart after acceptance can produce two deliveries with the same Message-ID. A failed final lease can also represent an accepted message.

Day 11 adds a durable audit entry in the same transaction when retry exhaustion or final lease expiry marks a job failed. It records the job ID, notification ID, attempt count and a sanitized reason. It leaves retry decisions unchanged. The staging rehearsal checks the audit and notification agree after the second rejected attempt. PostgreSQL runtime tests cover concurrent claims, pre-send interruption and lease recovery. The sink covers accepted delivery, authentication-required rejection, certificate failures, temporary refusal, synthetic recipient guards and stop after acceptance.

Operator reconciliation: locate the job and notification, check terminal audit and attempt count, correlate stable Message-ID with authorized relay logs, and record confirmed delivery, confirmed rejection or unknown. For an unknown outcome, obtain incident owner approval before redrive. Keep recipient, body, and raw transport credentials off incident exports. Mail operations must approve duplicate tolerance, deduplication or redrive policy before production.
